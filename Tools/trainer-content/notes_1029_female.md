## How this family was written

The README's family process, run with agents: one agent measured each rig
(angles, timing, props, contact points, paint, framing and the probed
joints), one swept the literature for all three, one drafted the three
entries, two independent reviewers audited them (evidence; model fidelity
and labels) and raised 46 problems between them, and one reviser worked
through every problem - 45 applied, 1 disproved with its own measurement -
and re-ran `validate()` and `validate_library()` on all three before they
were accepted. The complete finding log is in the round folder
(`drafts/female_log.md`); the measuring scripts are the round's own.

LABEL PLAN
Rows are the batch's squeezed set (0.50/0.68/0.86 become 0.48/0.64/0.80), all five pinned with `overrides`. This is the one entry in the family that had to be re-planned from scratch.

The draft's clear-space premise does not survive measurement. It read a 4x6 grid of quarter-columns ("the left strip u 0-0.25", "the top band v 0-0.17"), but a pill is 0.195-0.415 wide and 0.044 tall, so it overruns those cells by one and a half columns. Against a body-only matte and a machine matte split into static and moving parts, every one of the draft's five pills landed on something: depth 36.7% of its rect on the lifter (880-1611 px in every still), knees 21.1%, feet 16.0%, pad 14.7% on the lifter plus 54.6% on the moving carriage, grip 8.3% on the lifter at t0 plus 22.2% moving machine. Two of them swallowed their own joint dot: `Centre feet on the plate` at 24 characters spans u 0.035-0.450 and foot_R is pinned at (0.420, 0.819) inside it in all eight samples, and `Lower to a right angle` at 22 characters ends at exactly u 0.420 against a pelvis that reads u 0.418-0.425, leaving a 2 px leader. And the body's own column is u 0.259-0.725 - 0.275-0.620 at row 0.48, 0.340-0.725 at 0.64, 0.377-0.715 at 0.80 - not the u 0.41-0.70 the draft claimed; even the probed joint dots reach u 0.297.

So the budgets were re-measured per row, forbidding the lifter in any of the four stills and any machine part that moves (static hardware is allowed, the precedent the old grip pill already rested on). Maximum pill width and character count, from gen.py's own `width()`:

- row 0.16: leading none at all (she reaches u 0.018) / trailing 0.135 (4 characters)
- row 0.32: leading none (u 0.024) / trailing 0.135 (4 characters)
- row 0.48: leading 0.240 (12) / trailing 0.345 (19)
- row 0.64: leading 0.305 (16) / trailing 0.240 (12)
- row 0.80: leading 0.342 (19) / trailing 0.250 (12)

The top two rows cannot carry a pill, so all five sit on 0.48, 0.64 and 0.80 - six slots for five pills. The squeeze is also why: it lands the top row at v 0.138-0.182, straddling the v 0-0.17 band the draft measured.

The pairing is then forced by the leaders, not chosen. pad leads and grip trails on row 0.48 because upper_arm_L (u 0.530-0.537) sits left of hand_L (u 0.644-0.652) and both are above every row: coming from opposite edges in that order they converge without crossing, while swapping the sides flips their order in u and must cross - measured, they touch within 0.006 at v 0.48. Moving pad to a wider slot lower down fails too: 0.64 leading crosses the depth leader at sample 2, and 0.80 leading puts 7.5% of the pill on the lifter. That caps the pad label at the 12-character budget, and at 12 the pill clips her by 3 px, so it is 11: `Pads on top`. depth takes 0.64 leading for pelvis, knees 0.64 trailing for patella_L (whose dot reaches u 0.696, clear of a pill starting at 0.770) and feet 0.80 leading for foot_R, which the 18-character pill ends 0.058 short of.

Checked on the final squeezed values: no pill covers one body pixel in any still or one pixel of moving machine, no cue's own dot falls inside its own pill at any sample, every leader is at least 0.045 long at every sample, no leader crosses another at any sample, and no leader passes through another pill.

Glows: two, one per visible thigh, re-centred against the measured red pixels. The bright tier is one blob of 5152-5681 px covering glutes and quadriceps on both legs, and only 0, 2.8, 13.5 and 6.9 per cent of it sits above the pelvis in the four stills - so almost all the visible paint is thigh and the Gluteus Maximus row gets no glow of its own on this framing. The blob sweeps right and down through the descent (u 0.285-0.594 / v 0.424-0.640 at t0, u 0.296-0.723 / v 0.519-0.678 at t2), its left half centroid running (0.373, 0.522) to (0.409, 0.579) and its right half (0.561, 0.534) to (0.647, 0.581), so each centre is set between its own thigh's two positions: far thigh (0.385, 0.550) rx 0.07 ry 0.05, near thigh (0.590, 0.545) rx 0.06 ry 0.04. OPEN POINTS
1. **Gluteus medius and minimus are painted bright and get no row.** The two kickbacks in this same family read that identical three-glute group as two rows (Gluteus Maximus 0.84 plus Gluteus Medius 0.40), and the Machine Glute Press and Machine Glute Kickback did the same earlier this round (SampleData.swift 47855+ and 47944+, both verified). Every squat in the library instead reads it as one Gluteus Maximus row with gluteus medius among the stabilisers, which is what this entry does, because no source read this round supports a medius fraction in a straight-ahead squat (Paoli 2009 found gluteus maximus the only one of eight thigh muscles to differ by stance width; Hopkins 2024 found hip adductor moments unchanged by width, P = 0.414; Selkowitz 2016 found the superior gluteus maximus preferred only by abduction or external rotation). The owner should settle it one way for the whole library rather than per family.

2. **The glute fraction, 0.44 or 0.48.** Kept at the Hack Squat (Stances) Standard 0.44 because that is this machine family's own nearest neighbour - and that carry is now the only stated reason, because the draft's rationale was wrong. It argued that the higher figures go with a trunk that leans and this trunk does not; the library says otherwise. Leg Press carries Gluteus Maximus primary 0.48 (SampleData.swift 7033) with the back flat on a pad, and Vertical Leg Press, 45-Degree, Single-Leg and Narrow-Stance Leg Press all carry 0.48, Pendulum Squat 0.48 and V-Squat 0.45, every one of them a fixed path with little or no lean. So if the owner wants the machine-squat family consistent at about 0.48, this is the one row to move, and 0.44 is the outlier rather than the pattern.

3. **The hamstrings are unpainted, so this lift has one row fewer than two of its neighbours - but not all three.** Hack Squat (Stances) Standard (3139-3140) and Leg Press (7034) carry Hamstrings secondary 0.25; the standalone Hack Squat (7116-7119) carries NO hamstring row at all and keeps its glute row SECONDARY, which the draft got wrong and which weakens the case for calling this legend short. Either re-paint the hamstrings dim on this model, or accept the shorter legend - the expected direction on a fixed path is less hamstring, not more (Schwanbeck 2009, Svensson 2022).

4. **Only the Standard stance was measured.** The stance table at SampleData.swift 3184-3190 already carries Standard, High, Low, Wide and Narrow with no notes and no per-stance activation, and the other four models were not read this round. The per-stance rows are a separate job, and the evidence for them is in this round's brief (Paoli 2009 and Hopkins 2024 for a higher Wide glute fraction, Lee 2026 for the wide-stance vastus medialis effect and the 3.7 degree extra knee flexion at narrow, McCaw & Melrose 1999 and Escamilla 2001 for what must not be claimed).

5. **NEW: this framing cannot carry five pills at full length, and that is a camera problem, not a copy problem.** Rows 0.16 and 0.32 have no usable leading gutter at all (the lifter reaches u 0.018 and 0.024) and only four characters trailing, because the carriage sweeps the top of the frame through the rep. All five pills are squeezed onto the bottom three rows and three of the five labels are cut to 9, 11 and 15 characters - `Knees out`, `Pads on top`, `Lower to 90 deg` - against 23, 21 and 22 in the draft. Worth a look on the harness simulator: a yaw that put more background on one side would buy the top rows back and let the pad and knee-tracking labels say more.

6. **`props.py` and `gaps.py` cannot see this machine.** Both printed no props for it: their body filter excludes any path containing `_Mesh`, and every machine part here is named VHS_<part>_Mesh, so all 149 of them were filtered out as body geometry (`review2/count2.py` confirms: 149 non-body, 149 containing `_Mesh`, so props.py would see 0). Every prop figure above comes from `props2.py`, which selects props by hierarchy instead. The same bug hits the other two lifts in this family more quietly - it drops exactly the five ankle-cuff pieces on the Standing Hinge Kickback and nine meshes on the Incline Bench Cable Kickback - and will hit any other model whose equipment meshes carry a `_Mesh` suffix.

7. **`summ.py` is wrong about this model twice over, and the second one is new.** Its rep detection keys on elbow_L, which is constant at 72.090 degrees, so it printed 74 spurious crossings of floating-point noise, and its min/max timestamps for flat series are unreliable for the same reason. Worse, its trunk term is `atan2(v[2], v[1])` - the lean in Z - and this rig is turned a quarter turn, so on this model that measures SIDEWAYS lean, not forward lean. Its 0.00 to -0.00 degrees is therefore both noise and the wrong axis; the forward lean is a constant 0.49 degrees. All angles and timings in this entry come from `fix/rig.py`, `fix/hip.py` and `fix/dwell.py`, which step integer frames.

8. **NEW: the Gluteus Maximus row has no glow.** Both glows sit on thighs, because only 0-13.5% of the visible bright paint is above the pelvis - the glute paint is mostly behind the shorts and the carriage on this rear three-quarter framing. The entry says so plainly rather than implying a glute glow, but if the owner wants the row visually marked, that needs a different camera rather than a moved ellipse.

9. **A true hip angle is not available from this rig's `thigh_*` joints.** `thigh_L` and `thigh_R` sit at the hip joint itself, laterally offset from the pelvis with the same height and depth, so any angle computed at them against the pelvis is exactly 90 degrees in every frame on all three models. The hip figures here use the femur as thigh-to-shin, which is why this entry reports 160.5 to 115.5 degrees where `summ.py`'s chest-thigh-shin term reports 157.5 to 121.9. A probe note, not a content fix.

OPEN POINTS
1. **The primary Hamstrings row is a first for the library's kickbacks** and needs the owner's explicit OK. It follows this model's paint, which has no dim tier at all. The warrant changed in this pass, though, and that matters: the draft led on Stien 2021's 106.5% biceps femoris against 77.0% gluteus maximus, and the full text (PMC7919354) says in its statistical analyses section that paired t-tests compared amplitude BETWEEN EXERCISES and that no comparison was made between muscles within one exercise - so that contrast is untested, two values normalised to two different maximal efforts, with heavily overlapping confidence intervals. The promotion now leads on Orishimo & McHugh 2015, which did test hamstring against gluteus maximus inside the same exercise (p < 0.001 for resisted standing hip extension, the glider and the slider; the diver was null at p = 0.087). Stien remains as supporting evidence for the near-straight knee. Even so, this legend is a different shape from the Cable Glute Kickback, Cable Knee-Drive Kickback and Machine Glute Kickback, all of which keep Hamstrings secondary.

2. **No dim and no faint tier anywhere on this model.** `tiers.py` re-run 2026-10-29 prints six bright muscles on the working side and empty arrays for both other tiers. Deliberate, or an export that lost its secondary tier? The Incline Bench Cable Kickback, exported the same day from the same folder, does have a dim tier, which makes the question worth asking before this ships - and it is the other half of that entry's open point 1, where the two models' hamstring tiers look swapped.

3. **The paint moves much further than its own size.** Its pixel centroid travels from (0.510, 0.494) to (0.653, 0.415) on a blob of 123-188 pixels, so no small ellipse can sit on it at both ends, and the single glow is now 0.165 by 0.09 - large for one muscle group - to cover 100 per cent of it in all four stills. If the owner would rather have a tighter glow pinned to one phase, the alternative is about 0.09 by 0.06 centred on either centroid, which is on paint at that phase and largely off it at the other. Worth showing side by side.

4. **thigh_L is a single static point in joints.json** at (0.513, 0.409) although the thigh plainly swings through the clip; only patella_L, shin_L and foot_L track the motion. Anything anchored on thigh_L sits still at the hip, which is why the glow is anchored on the knee end of its pair. A probe fix, not a content fix - and the same gap is why the Incline Bench Cable Kickback's soft glow needed re-centring.

5. **RESOLVED: the weight stack rises 54 cm.** The draft recorded `props.py` and `brief.py` disagreeing (23.3 cm against 54 cm) and printed neither. `props2.py`'s 17-sample scan settles it at 54.0 cm, and the same pass corrects the cuff cluster from `props.py`'s 45.2-49.5 cm to 109.2-127.9 cm - which the rig independently confirms, since the working ankle's own straight-line excursion is 1.29 m. Both figures are now in the header bullet. `props.py` differences only t0 and t 3.18 s, and 3.18 s lands partway down a descent.

6. **The torso pill sits on the machine frame.** The left column is machine from top to bottom (28.8-37.9% at the two top bands), so there is no clear left-hand anchor above v 0.713 and one pill has to go there. Measured, it is 25.1% over static machine, 0% over the lifter and 0% over anything that moves - static hardware rather than a moving part, but it is the one pill in this family that is not on measured empty space. Note the draft's prose and open point named the HIPS pill for this; the code had it the other way round and the code is right, because chest sits left of pelvis and that is the order that keeps the two top leaders from crossing.

7. **The hinge is 67.5 degrees, well past the 40 degrees Aygun-Polat measured,** and that study measured a Bulgarian split squat rather than a kickback. The copy now names both facts and adds a third the draft left out - that trunk flexion raised the hamstrings as much as the glute (biceps femoris 14.46 to 20.98 %MVIC, p = 0.015, against the glute's 16.25 to 21.32), so it is not a glute-specific effect. The owner may still prefer the study dropped from the cue altogether rather than cited with three caveats.

8. **`summ.py` is unusable on this clip and its hip term is not a hip angle.** Every static joint prints min = max at two different timestamps, the elbow_L midpoint-crossing list runs to 84 entries on a joint that never moves, and its hip is `ang(chest, thigh, shin)`, which the knee feeds straight into - at the start of this rep the knee is 26 degrees bent, so its 104.413 is a composite. A pelvis-thigh angle cannot be computed on any of these three rigs: `thigh_L` and `thigh_R` sit at the hip joint itself, so an angle measured there against the pelvis reads exactly 90.000 in every frame.

OPEN POINTS
1. **The two models' hamstring tiers look swapped, and this is the family's sharpest open question.** Here the hamstrings are painted dim although the working knee reaches 172 degrees at the finish - and the two usable sources tie a near-straight knee to MORE hamstring and less glute (Shin & Yoo 2025: biceps femoris and the BF/Gmax ratio both significantly higher in the knee-extended version; Macadam & Feser 2019 pooled: prone knee-flexed 18.6% against knee-extended 15.5% for gluteus maximus) - while the Standing Hinge Kickback, whose knee never passes 170 degrees, paints them bright. If anything the tiers should be the other way round. Flagged rather than overridden, because the rows follow the paint: this entry keeps Hamstrings secondary at 0.50 and the standing one has it primary at the same 0.50. One of the two exports probably wants a look. Note for the record that the draft rested part of this case on Song & Jeon 2026, which cannot carry it: that study is a banded reciprocal-inhibition protocol with no straight-knee condition, and its glute result is non-monotonic (highest at 90 degrees of knee flexion, lower at both 60 and 120).

2. **The quadriceps are unpainted although the knee opens 92 degrees under cable load.** Named among the stabilisers instead of given a row, exactly as the Machine Glute Press needed earlier this round. Either re-paint them dim or keep the legend as it is - but the same question has now come up twice in one round on two different models.

3. **RESOLVED, but worth the owner's eye.** What the resting leg rests on is now measured (`fix/nearprop.py`): its knee is on the bench pad at 0.008 m, and its foot touches nothing at all - the nearest mesh to foot_L is 0.227 m away - so the leg lies along the pad with the foot hanging free past the end. The setup step and The Other Leg cue now say exactly that. The owner should confirm it is what the model is meant to show, because a foot hanging in mid-air is an odd thing to coach and a real lifter would usually have it somewhere.

4. **RESOLVED: the stills were scanned for red pixels after all.** This was the one lift in the family whose glow centres rested on the joint projections alone, and the scan found a real problem - the soft glow covered none of the dim hamstring paint at the apex and the bright glute paint is entirely occluded there. Both glows are now measured; the full one was kept and the soft one re-centred and widened. The occlusion itself is the remaining question: should a row whose paint disappears at the top of the rep carry a glow at all?

5. **The trailing pill at the top row overlaps the static cable tower in its right third.** It is the only right-hand anchor the framing has, since the tower fills u 0.80-1 from v 0.10 to v 0.70 and both middle rows are measured unusable (row 0.48 leading has three characters of clear space, trailing none). Static hardware rather than the lifter or a moving part, but it is not measured empty space.

6. **`nearest.py` cannot name a right-side joint on this lift.** Its joint list is hardcoded left-only (`JS = [thigh_L, patella_L, shin_L, foot_L, toe_L, pelvis]`, line 17), so run as shipped it reports the ankle cuff nearest toe_L at 0.21 m and foot_L at 0.24 m, which reads as if the cuff were on the left leg. It is not - with both sides in the list the answer is unambiguous at 0.01 m from foot_R. Every cuff and clip figure here comes from `nearest_both.py`. The same gap bit the Standing Hinge Kickback in this round's draft, which named thigh_L as the cable's nearest joint at the top when it is really shin_R on the standing leg. `nearall.py` has the same hole (both arms, left leg only). A tools fix for the round folder.

7. **The knee and foot separations are not stance widths.** They are 3D distances dominated by the Z axis: at the bottom ankle_L is [0.060, 0.692, 0.579] and ankle_R [0.332, 0.386, -0.294], so dz 0.873 of the 0.965 m total against dx 0.272. The gap shrinks to 0.248 m at the top because the working leg swings back alongside the resting one, not because the feet come together, so no copy anywhere uses those figures. The draft's version of this point claimed no copy anywhere used them, which was true here but not of the sibling entry, where the ankle pair appeared in two cues, the comparison note and the header bullet; those are now gone too.

8. **`summ.py` miscounts this clip and mistimes its holds.** It prints 191 samples and a 3.98 s period for a clip that has 192 integer frames and an exact 4.00 s period (a 96-frame lag reproduces every joint position to 0.000000 m), because it advances by fps*0.0417 = 1.0008 frames. Its sample count is never the frame count, and flat series get arbitrary min/max timestamps. All timings here come from `fix/rig.py`, `fix/dwell.py` and `fix/period.py`.
