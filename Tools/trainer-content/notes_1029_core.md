## How this family was written

Unlike the first two families of the round, this one went through the
README's full agent process: for each lift one agent measured the rig and
one swept the literature, a third drafted the entry from both, two
independent reviewers audited it (evidence; model fidelity and labels), a
reviser worked through every finding, and a final skeptic re-measured and
re-read every citation before the entry was accepted. Each lift's complete
finding log - what was applied, what was applied with a change and what was
disproved, with the proof - is kept beside this file in the round folder
(`drafts/<slug>_rev_log.md` and `drafts/<slug>_final_log.md`). The
measuring scripts are the round's own (`brief.py`, `summ.py`, `props.py`,
`nearest.py`, `gaps.py`, `palm.py`, `trainer_shot.py`, `overlay.py`).

What the process caught, as examples: a stretch-range study quoted with its
ranges reversed (the growth advantage was below a flat foot, which this
model has no block to reach), an EMG paper used to rank two muscles when it
normalised each to its own maximum, a header bullet that put the camera
behind the lifter when it is in front, a glow that sat off the painted
muscle, and several figures that were right to two decimals in a script
printout but wrong at full precision.

OPEN POINTS
OPEN POINTS
1. Rectus Abdominis fraction (judgement call, owner's to make). The paint is bright on all three abs meshes, so the row is primary under the house rule, and it carries Cable Wood Chop's 0.44. The cost is that Cable Wood Chop is the least comparable neighbour in the library - a dynamic diagonal chop with trunk flexion and rotation - while every cable lift that resists or produces rotation uses 0.30 .secondary (Reverse Cable Wood Chop, Cable Rotation, Cable Side Bend), Side Plank, the closest anti-movement isometric, uses 0.22 .secondary, and the only study cited for the row says the rectus is little activated. Three options side by side: (a) 0.44 .primary, following the paint, as written; (b) 0.30 .secondary, following the cable neighbours, which drops the second primary row entirely because 0.30 would otherwise be a new lowest primary in the core set (the floor is Farmer Carry March's Obliques 0.35); (c) 0.40 .primary, splitting them and keeping two primary rows without going below the floor.

2. No deep-core row. The rig has no transverse abdominis mesh, so the paint cannot rank it and it sits among the stabilisers, as on Side Plank and Russian Twist. The one library precedent for giving it a row is Cable Wood Chop, which carries Transverse Abdominis as a .secondary abs row at 0.40 - the only such row in the whole file. Note for whoever reads this next: that entry's own comment calls the row legend-only, but it is not, in the sense common_1_50.py uses - LEGEND_ONLY is just {Hip Flexors, Serratus Anterior}, and part_of matches on abdomin, so the app files the row under abs like any other. Whether the Pallof press should match Cable Wood Chop is the owner's call: there is no measurement either way, and the one Pallof EMG abstract publishes no values.

3. Paint against anatomy. The elbows extend 111.6 degrees and the stack lifts 15.7 cm, so the shoulders and triceps are plainly working, and the legs and hips hold the stance against a sideways pull, yet nothing outside the abs is painted. Following the house rule they are named among the stabilisers (anterior deltoid, triceps brachii, gluteus medius, erector spinae). Rows for them would need dim paint added to the model.

4. Framing. At yaw -0.3 the press runs almost down the camera axis: the hands travel 32.5 cm forward in the model (33.2 cm in a straight line) but only about 49 px on screen for hand_L (41 px across, 27 px down) and 33 px for hand_R (20 px across, 27 px down), with forearm_L moving about 80 px (65 across, 47 down) - most of it sideways rather than along the press line. The two pressed-out stills sit 1 px apart, so two of the four stills are near-duplicates; t0 is the bottom frame, 48 px away, and t3 a genuine mid-press frame 24 px from it. At the top the arms foreshorten into a flat horizontal line that reads as a lateral hold rather than a forward press. For comparison, three of the round's four other cable lifts are shot side-on (Lying Cable Curl -1.3, Cable Reverse Curl -1.4, Incline Bench Cable Kickback -1.3) and the fourth three-quarter (Bent-Over Cable Overhead Triceps Extension -0.6). If the owner wants the press to read in the trainer, the model wants re-framing with more yaw - which would move all five labels.

5. Hand roll and slide, a model artifact to flag rather than describe in the copy. The bar is rigid and fixed in height, but the hands do not hold it the way a real grip would: each wrist crosses from 2.3 cm below the bar's centre line at the chest to 2.7 cm above it pressed out, and the hand gap closes from 0.3368 m to 0.2400 m, so each hand slides about 4.8 cm inward along a 0.36 m bar. The wrist's own angle barely moves through this (161.4 degrees at the chest, 162.8 pressed out, a 3.6-degree range over the whole clip), so it is the hands rolling and sliding around the bar, not the wrist giving. Nothing in the copy depends on it; it is worth a look if the model is ever re-exported.

OPEN POINTS
1) Legend length. Following the paint to the letter gives ten rows (4 primary, 6 secondary). That would be a first: counting all 443 ExerciseContent blocks in SampleData.swift, the longest shipped is nine (cleanAndPressContent, chestSupportedRowMachineContent). The viewport legend is one line per rank, names joined by a middle dot with lineLimit(1) at 9.5 pt mono (Exercise3DView.swift:507-566), so the primary line - OBLIQUES, HAMSTRINGS, GLUTEUS MAXIMUS, GLUTEUS MEDIUS, 56 characters - will be clipped on the phone, and the muscle panel still shows all ten. That clipping is routine rather than a defect, and more clearly so than the draft said: rebuilding every primary line from the 443 shipped blocks, 46 of them are already longer than 56 characters, up to 102 (platePinchHold, towelGripHold), with cleanAndPress at 96, sumoDeadlift at 84 and this round's own Cable Reverse Curl at 81. The SECONDARY line is the worse one, and the draft was right that both lines get cut: ERECTOR SPINAE, LATERAL DELTOID, ANTERIOR DELTOID, TRAPEZIUS, ROTATOR CUFF, RECTUS ABDOMINIS is 97 characters over six names, and nothing shipped has a secondary line longer than 79 (weightedPullUp and the five pulldowns), so this would be the library's longest. So the ten rows, not the line length, are what the owner is really deciding. If a trim is wanted, the cheapest one with a precedent behind it is Anterior Deltoid: the Overhead Carry, the nearest overhead-hold neighbour, carries the anterior deltoid and the rotator cuff as stabilisers rather than rows, and moving the anterior deltoid alone brings this to nine rows, leaves the primary line untouched and brings the secondary line to 78 characters, just inside the shipped maximum. The Rotator Cuff row cannot simply be dropped the way the draft suggested - the README's 101-200 rule (lines 763-766) makes a painted cuff a row at the paint's rank. My call was to follow the paint and flag it.

2) Obliques leading at 0.62 above bright glutes and hamstrings at 0.50 is a judgement call, and the two kinds of evidence point different ways. For the lead: the app's own library row for this lift is primaryMuscle OBLIQUES (SampleData.swift:1489-1491), the paint makes both obliques bright, and the only measured one-arm-load numbers available are a suitcase carry's (external oblique 33.0% MVIC, above longissimus 29.1 and rectus abdominis 18.9; Ellestad 2024), with Lyons 2017 putting the contralateral external oblique higher in the one-arm snatch and clean than in the swing. Against it: StrengthLog's windmill guide, the only practice source that exists for this lift, lists Primary glutes, front deltoids, lateral deltoids, rear deltoids, hamstrings and Secondary lower back, abs, rotator cuff, lats, traps - it never names the obliques, and it ranks exactly the alternative the owner would be choosing. There is no windmill EMG of any kind to settle it. If the owner would rather the hinge lead, Gluteus Maximus and Hamstrings move above the obliques and the comment changes with them.

3) Trapezius 0.36 instead of the Suitcase Carry's 0.44. That library row quotes Bordelon et al. 2021 Table 2 (upper trapezius 21-28% MVIC, lower 8-10%), which could not be re-read this round - the Europe PMC record carries no abstract and the publisher full text returned HTTP 402. The vertical presses' estimate is used instead so no unverified number is repeated, and it now also sits under a readable neighbour, the Overhead Carry's Upper Trapezius 0.60. If someone opens that full text, both this row and the Suitcase Carry's own citation want revisiting.

4) Possible authored clamps. elbow_L (175.0000-175.0001), knee_L (176.9994-177.0005), anklegap (0.3622) and shouldergap (0.3922) are flat to within float noise across all 192 frames, and the foot yaw is a constant -42.00 degrees on both feet. Three of those carry cue copy (the straight arm, the long legs, the turned feet). The copy describes what the model shows either way, but the owner may want to know those series never vary.

5) Stance width and the size of the foot turn. The turn itself is settled - both feet yaw 42 degrees away from the bell side all clip, which is what StrengthLog teaches, so it is now in the setup and the legs cue, and the draft's square-feet reading was wrong. Two small gaps remain: the model stands 0.36 m at the ankles against 0.39 m at the shoulder joints, where the guide says slightly wider than shoulder width, and 42 degrees is a good deal more than the guide's slightly. The copy says shoulder-width and turned away without a number, which fits both; if the owner would rather the setup match the guide exactly it would need to say slightly wider.

6) No ghosts for this lift, as for the arms and glutes families of this round, so every mistake view falls back to the red ring. The hips, bell, legs and head cues are all position cues that would each take one if the owner wants them.

7) Three scripts to fix before they are trusted on another clip. props.py under-reports prop travel here (9-12 cm against the true 60 cm) because it samples two near-identical standing poses, and nearhand.py has the same flaw, sampling frac 0.00 and 0.45 at the same standing pose. period.py's bottom-hold grouping is also wrong: its local-minimum test does not collapse a flat run, so on this clip it returns the non-contiguous [42, 44, 46, 47, 49, 138, 140, 142, 143, 145] and the round read that as 42-49; per frame the minimum is held over 42-50 and 138-146. The copy now uses a per-frame read (close/kin3.py) for the frame ranges and brief.py only for positions. Related: brief.py and summ.py step by fps * 0.0417 = 1.0008 frames, which drifts about a frame over the clip - that drift is what produced the 1.42 s descent and it should be a flat 1.0.
