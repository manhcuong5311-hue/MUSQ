## 0. Bottom line

1. **There is no EMG study of the burpee.** Europe PMC: 65 records match burpee + EMG/muscle-activity
   terms and 129 records carry "burpee" in the title or abstract; none measures muscle activity during
   a burpee. Gist et al. 2014, the key burpee physiology paper, says outright that whole-body muscle
   activation data were not available to them. A 2026 open data set (Kotolova et al.) records EMG of
   four muscles while people do burpees but publishes no analysed or normalised levels. The only other
   "burpee EMG" claim found is an unpublished test reported by Les Mills (no numbers, commercial
   source). **So every activation fraction for the Burpee is an estimate or a value carried from the
   library's push-up / squat jump / plank / mountain climber rows, and the Swift comment must say so.**
2. **One burpee-specific biomechanics paper exists**: Di Pietro & Di Puccio 2024 (ISBS conference
   proceedings, predicted ground reaction forces, not a journal article). Abstract only (the PDF is
   behind a Cloudflare check): the knees were the most loaded joints, highest at the landing,
   about 3.5 body weights (a model prediction).
3. **Energy cost / heart rate is well covered**: Gist 2014 (full text: 30-s all-out burpee sets
   reached about 85% of peak heart rate and 78% of peak oxygen uptake, i.e. vigorous by ACSM's
   bands), Ratamess 2015 (sets of 10 burpees cost more oxygen than heavy squats, deadlifts or lunges),
   Poulios 2024 (burpees were the most demanding of seven bodyweight exercises).
4. **Technique and faults** come from organisations: NASM (exercise library page, the most complete
   one: 4 steps, muscles, 5 common mistakes), CrossFit (step-by-step, 3 named faults), StrengthLog
   (7 steps and muscles inside two guide articles; StrengthLog has no stand-alone burpee page),
   ACE (no standard bodyweight burpee entry; two expert/press articles and the push-up page), NSCA
   (a pedagogy article that phases the burpee, and a warm-up sheet). ExRx could not be read
   (Cloudflare 403 direct and in the browser; the Wayback Machine returned 429 every time).
   ACSM has nothing on burpees beyond the intensity bands Gist uses.
5. **Model checks that limit the copy** (section 5): the back ROUNDS at the bottom (about 30 degrees
   more flexion between pelvis, chest and neck than when standing), the top landing is toes-first
   on nearly straight knees that then bend, the jump launches from a half squat after the hands
   leave the floor (not from the deep squat), and the feet land narrower than the hands. Do not
   write copy that contradicts these.

## 1. How to do it, phase by phase (sources against the model)

Model phases (brief): stand -> squat, hands down -> jump both feet back to a straight plank ->
full push-up, chest close to the floor -> jump both feet in to a deep squat, hands still down ->
hands up, rise -> jump straight up, arms overhead -> land, absorb, stand. 3 reps of 5.33 s.

Elbow angle: no burpee source gives one. The library already teaches about 45 degrees on push-ups
(labels "Elbows ~45° from the body" x5, "Elbows ~45°" x11); the model measures 49 at the bottom.

Push-up load (useful for a "why"):
- Regular push-up peak ground reaction force under the hands = 0.64 +/- 0.04 of body mass
  (23 recreationally fit adults, 14 men / 9 women; hands at shoulder width; 2-s cadence).
  Wurm, VanderZanden, Spadavecchia, Durocher, Bickham, Petushek & Ebben, ISBS 2010 proceedings
  (full text read, Table 1), published as Ebben et al. 2011, J Strength Cond Res 25(10):2891-2894.
  Bent-knee 0.49; feet on a 30.48 cm box 0.70, on 60.96 cm 0.74; hands on 30.48 cm 0.55, 60.96 cm 0.41.
- Static support: 69.16% of body mass at the top and 75.04% at the bottom of a traditional
  push-up; knees-down 53.56% / 61.80% (28 highly strength-trained men). Suprak, Dawes & Stephenson
  2011, J Strength Cond Res 25(2):497-503 (abstract read; the numbers are from the Cooper Institute
  write-up and StrengthLog, Richter 2024, which gives about 70% as a rule of thumb).

### 1.6 Land and go again
| Claim | Source | Model |
|---|---|---|
| Land softly with bent knees, then get ready for the next rep | NASM step 4 | knees bend from about 170 at touchdown to about 120, then stand: matches |
| Land softly and flow into the next rep with no break | StrengthLog step 7 | stands fully (by about 5.29 s), next rep at 5.33 s |
| Not landing and resetting properly before the next rep raises the risk of falling | NASM mistake 5 | the model resets to tall standing every rep |
| Unlike burpees, which usually feature a hard, violent landing, ACE's alternative stresses a controlled, soft landing | ACE, Ross 2015 | |

Landing evidence (the library already uses the first two in Box Jump, Step-Up Jump, Squat Jump):
- Myers et al. 2011, Am J Sports Med 39(8):1714-1722 (abstract; PMC4167636): 16 healthy adults
  (6 men, 10 women), 40 cm drop; peak vertical force 1.63 +/- 0.73 body weights soft vs
  2.60 +/- 1.32 stiff (P < .001); knee flexion range 55.4 vs 36.8 degrees. Tibial translation and
  rotation did not differ. -> soft is about 37% lower.
- Guy-Cherry et al. 2018, Sports Med Int Open 2(2):E35-E40 (abstract; PMC6225960): 30 adults, 40 cm
  drop jumps; force 1.34 (soft, about 60 degrees of knee bend), 1.50 (self-selected), 1.81 (stiff)
  body weights; EMG of gluteus maximus, quadriceps, hamstrings, tibialis anterior and gastrocnemius
  did NOT differ between styles (p > 0.66); reactive strength was higher when stiff (0.93 vs 0.64).
  -> about 26% lower. Library wording "about a third" covers both.
- Devita & Skelly 1992, Med Sci Sports Exerc 24(1):108-115 (abstract): 8 female athletes, 59 cm;
  soft 117 vs stiff 77 degrees of knee flexion; the hip and knee muscles absorbed more energy in
  the soft landing (hip -0.60 vs -0.39 W/kg; knee -0.89 vs -0.61 W/kg), the ankle more in the stiff
  one. -> "a soft landing lets the hip and knee muscles take the landing".
- Milner et al. 2012, Knee 19(4):399-403 (abstract): telling female recreational athletes to land
  softly lowered peak vertical force and increased knee bend; telling them to land with the knees
  over the toes also increased knee bend.
- Landing Error Scoring System (LESS) item definitions: Padua et al. 2015, J Athl Train
  50(6):589-595, Table 1 (full text, PMC4527442); validity: Padua et al. 2009, Am J Sports Med
  37(10):1996-2002 (2691 subjects). Errors include: knee bent less than 30 degrees at first contact;
  landing heel-to-toe or flat-footed; patella inside the mid-foot (knee caving); feet wider OR
  narrower than shoulder width; knee bending less than 45 degrees from contact to its deepest point;
  a stiff landing (little trunk, hip and knee give). Model: toe-to-heel (pass), knees out (pass),
  about 50 degrees of knee bend after contact (pass), knees nearly straight at first contact
  (would score as an error), feet hip-width (would score "narrow"). So cite LESS only for toe-first,
  knees out and letting the knees bend; never for stance width or a bent knee at contact.
- Di Pietro & Di Puccio 2024, ISBS Proceedings Archive 42(1): art. 261 (abstract only): burpee with
  push-up, motion capture plus PREDICTED hand and foot reaction forces; the knees were the most
  loaded joints, especially at landing, reaching about 3.5 body weights. Method details, sample and
  every other number could not be read.

## 3. Muscle activity

### 3.1 Who names which muscles (organisations)
- **NASM** (Squat Thrust Burpees): primary quadriceps (squat and jump), pectoralis major (push-up),
  glutes (hip extension in the jump); secondary triceps, deltoids, core stabilizers, hip flexors.
- **StrengthLog** (Abelsson 2025, both guide articles): primary chest, front deltoid, quads, glutes,
  adductors; secondary abs, calves, triceps. Its text also says the burpee hits the chest, triceps,
  abs, hip flexors and legs.
- **CrossFit** (2019): upper-body pushing muscles (shoulders, chest, triceps), which also absorb the
  impact when lowering to the floor; core (abdominals and erectors), which hold the trunk and also
  move through flexion and extension; lower body (quadriceps, glutes, hamstrings, calves).
- Gist 2014 (full text): no muscle data; assumes more whole-body muscle is active than in cycling.

Against the paint: bright Chest + Quads = NASM's primary pair (minus glutes). Every dim group is
named by at least one organisation: abs (StrengthLog, CrossFit, NASM core), obliques (only as core /
abdominals; measured in push-ups by Freeman 2006), front delt (StrengthLog, NASM deltoids, CrossFit
shoulders), triceps (all three), hip flexors (NASM; StrengthLog text), hamstrings (CrossFit),
calves (StrengthLog, CrossFit).

### 3.2 Evidence for the parts (no burpee EMG exists)
Push-up (the burpee holds one full push-up per rep):
- Youdas et al. 2010, J Strength Cond Res 24(12):3352-3362 (abstract): 20 adults (11 men, 9 women),
  peak EMG as %MVIC across three hand widths, with and without handles: triceps 73-109, pectoralis
  major 95-105, serratus anterior 67-87, posterior deltoid 11-21.
- Kang et al. 2014, J Phys Ther Sci 26(6):909-910 (full-text table, PMC4085219): 15 young men,
  standard push-up, mean %MVIC: clavicular pec 79.1 +/- 18.0, sternocostal pec 64.5 +/- 18.3,
  serratus anterior 45.5 +/- 19.4, rectus abdominis 21.8 +/- 14.5.
- Freeman, Karpowicz, Gray & McGill 2006, Med Sci Sports Exerc 38(3):570-577 (full text, Table 1):
  10 participants (9 men, 1 woman), peak %MVC, standard push-up: right pectoralis major 61.2,
  right anterior deltoid 42.0, right triceps 66.0, rectus abdominis 24.9 right / 20.1 left,
  external oblique 22.5 / 24.7, internal oblique 21.6 / 26.6, latissimus 10.6 / 14.1, erector
  spinae 4.6 / 2.7, biceps 4.4. Ballistic (clapping) push-up: pec 88.9, anterior deltoid 60.8,
  triceps 88.6, rectus abdominis 56.7 / 41.0, external oblique 55.8 / 63.0. (Ballistic push-ups
  roughly double the abdominal activity; the burpee's jump-back catch is a different ballistic
  event, so this is context, not a burpee value.)
- Cogley et al. 2005, J Strength Cond Res 19(3):628-633 (abstract): narrow hands > wide for pec
  and triceps EMG (40 subjects). Marcolin et al. 2015, J Athl Train 50(11):1126-1132 (abstract):
  8 volunteers, 12 muscles; narrow base highest for triceps and pec; least activity while lowering.
  Calatayud et al. 2015, J Strength Cond Res 29(1):246-253 (abstract): push-up and bench press at
  matched EMG gave similar strength gains.
Plank: Schoenfeld et al. 2014 (Sports Biomech 13(3):296-306) and Snarr & Esco 2014 (J Strength
Cond Res 28(11):3298-3305) compare plank variations; abstracts give no levels usable here.
Jump and landing: no %MVIC exists for a bodyweight squat jump (the library's jump family notes say
the same). Guy-Cherry 2018 recorded gluteus maximus, quadriceps, hamstrings, tibialis anterior and
gastrocnemius in landings (no style difference, no level usable). Phillips, Paletta & Perlet 2025,
Int Biomech 12(1):1-6: MVIC underestimated peak drop-jump activity by 71-140% in six leg muscles,
so no %MVIC from a jumping task is turned into a fraction (library rule).
Hip flexors: no measurement in any burpee or squat-thrust study. The jump-in is fast hip flexion
from the plank (hip about 164) to the deep squat (53) in one hop; that is the movement the library
already credits to the hip flexors on the Mountain Climber (ExRx iliopsoas, via
mountainClimberContent).
Other: Kotolova et al. 2026, Data Brief 66:112797 (PMC13156998): burpees (40 s on / 20 s off)
recorded with EMG of biceps, triceps, rectus femoris and lateral gastrocnemius in 20 adults; raw
data set only, no analysed levels. Not usable.

## 4. Energy cost, heart rate, pacing, injury

- **Gist, Freese & Cureton 2014**, J Strength Cond Res 28(11):3033-3040, doi 10.1519/JSC.0000000000000522,
  PMID 24832968 (full text). 11 moderately trained ROTC students (8 men, 3 women; peak VO2 54.1);
  4 x 30 s all-out burpees (squat, hands down, kick back, push-up, feet in, leap up with arms
  overhead) with 4 min active recovery. Mean 77.6 +/- 6.9% of peak VO2 and 84.6 +/- 5.3% of peak
  heart rate (sprint cycling 80.4% and 86.8%, not significantly different); RPE 14.5 (hard);
  lactate 3.7 to 8.3 mmol/L across bouts. Classed vigorous by ACSM's bands (Garber 2011: 64-90% of
  max VO2, 77-95% of max heart rate, RPE 14-17). No muscle data.
- **Ratamess et al. 2015**, J Strength Cond Res 29(1):47-57, PMID 24942174 (abstract). 10
  resistance-trained men; 3 x 10 burpees, 2-min rests; mean VO2 over work and rest 22.9 +/- 2.1
  ml/kg/min, second only to battling ropes (24.6), above squats (19.6), deadlifts (18.9) and lunges
  (17.3) at 75% 1RM and the push-up with lateral crawl (19.5); plank lowest (7.9). The abstract
  does not describe the burpee version.
- **Poulios et al. 2024**, J Strength Cond Res 38(12):2034-2046, PMID 39178048 (abstract). 10 young
  adults, seven bodyweight exercises for 30 s and 45 s: burpees highest for RPE, mean heart rate,
  lactate, METs and EPOC, plank lowest. Total energy expenditure (kcal/min, 30 s / 45 s): burpees
  32.2 / 40.7; push-ups 22.0 / 24.9; jumping jacks 23.1 / 22.8; squat 19.9 / 23.2; single-leg squat
  19.8 / 21.2; forward lunge 18.6 / 22.0; plank 11.3 / 12.6.
- **Podstawski et al. 2019**, J Hum Kinet 69:137-147, PMC6815084 (full text). 3-Minute Burpee Test
  norms in 9833 students aged 18-25: men 56.69, women 48.84 cycles in 3 min (about 19 and 16 a
  minute). THIS test burpee has no push-up and no jump (stand up, arms overhead, clap). Test rule:
  plank on straight arms without arching the back, legs fully straight. Cites Podstawski 2016a:
  the test raised heart rate to an average 181.92 b/min in female students.
- **McRae et al. 2012**, Appl Physiol Nutr Metab 37(6):1124-1131 (abstract): 22 recreationally
  active women, 4 weeks; one set of 8 x 20 s of one exercise per session (burpees, jumping jacks,
  mountain climbers or squat thrusts), 10 s rest: peak VO2 up about 8% (treadmill group about 7%)
  plus large muscular-endurance gains (push-ups +135%).
- **Gist et al. 2015**, Mil Med 180(5):492-498 (abstract): 4 weeks of 4-7 x 30 s all-out burpees
  maintained cadets' fitness (no change vs normal training). **Perez-Ifran et al. 2024**,
  J Strength Cond Res 38(1):10-20 (abstract; erratum 38(6):1005-1006): 10 x 4 s all-out burpees
  improved countermovement jump height (d = 0.72); sprint intervals raised heart rate and RPE more.
- **2024 Adult Compendium of Physical Activities** (pacompendium.com, Conditioning Exercise): code
  02020 calisthenics incl. burpees, vigorous, 7.5 MET; code 02214 high-intensity interval exercise
  incl. burpees, vigorous, 11.0 MET. Assigned group values, not a measurement of this movement (the
  jump family declined Compendium codes for the same reason).
- **Pacing / form under fatigue**: NASM (quality before quantity; fatigue degrades form and raises
  injury risk; short sets with full rest), ACE (Ross 2015; McCall as reported: speed not needed),
  StrengthLog (best as HIIT or a finisher, not traditional sets of 8-10), CrossFit (slow down to
  keep full range). The model's 5.33 s per rep (about 11 a minute) is a controlled pace, slower
  than test pace (about 16-19 a minute in the 3-MBT).
- **Injury**: Batterson et al. 2020, Mayo Clin Proc 95(3):468-475, PMID 31813529 (abstract): 100
  adults (82% women) in 6-week group strength-endurance classes; 24 injuries in 18 people, 9.0 per
  1000 training hours; knees (7) and back (6) most often hurt; burpees and squats were the most
  common movements causing injury. Rynecki et al. 2019 (J Sports Med Phys Fitness 59(7):1206-1212):
  US emergency-department injuries tied to HIIT-type exercises (burpees, push-ups, lunges, boxes,
  kettlebells) rose 144% from 2007-2011 to 2012-2016 (not burpee-specific).

## 5. Model checks made while researching (frames.json, rep 1)

These are facts about the animation that decide which source claims can be used. Keep them out of
user copy as numbers.
1. **The back rounds at the bottom.** Angle pelvis-chest-neck: 170.7 standing and in the plank,
   140.7 at the hand touch (0.96 s) and 140.6 in the jump-in squat (3.50 s): about 30 degrees more
   trunk flexion. So no "keep your back flat as you reach down" cue, and no ROUNDED BACK ghost.
2. **Top landing**: toes touch at 4.75 s with the knee at about 170 (rig straight 167) and the
   ankle still about 6 cm up; heels down by 4.92 s (about 0.17 s later); knees bottom at about 120
   (4.96 s), hips 140, trunk 15 degrees forward; knees 34 cm apart vs ankles 24 cm. Copy can say
   land on the balls of the feet and let the heels settle and the knees bend; it should not say
   land with the knees already bent.
3. **Jump-in landing**: toes at 3.42 s, heels down by 3.50 s, deep squat knees 89 / hips 53, knees
   35 cm apart vs ankles 24 cm, feet 26 cm behind the hands; feet narrower than the hands.
4. **The jump starts from a half squat, chest up**: after the jump-in the hands leave the floor
   (from about 3.54 s), the trunk comes up while the knees open to about 122 (pelvis 10 cm below
   standing, trunk 13-18 degrees forward) around 4.00-4.08 s with the arms swinging forward and up;
   the heels start to rise at about 4.13 s and the feet leave at about 4.35 s. Gist's "leap from the
   squat" fits loosely; do not write "jump straight out of the deep squat with the hands on the floor".
5. **Jump back**: feet together, about 0.25 s airborne, land 27 cm apart on straight knees (about
   168), hips on the line at once.
6. **Push-up**: hands 54 cm apart (1.38 x the 39 cm shoulder-joint width) and directly under the
   shoulders; elbows 47 at the bottom, upper arms 49 degrees from the body; body line 180 at the
   bottom; neck slightly flexed against standing (chest-neck-head 164-166 vs 175) but in line with
   the trunk.
7. **Tempo**: 3 reps in 16 s, one every 5.33 s; the lifter stands fully between reps.

What the model does NOT do (do not describe): step back or forward, clap, let the hips or thighs
touch the floor, land the feet as wide as the hands, tuck the knees in the air, travel forward,
pause in the squat for long, flat back at the bottom.

Copy-safe phrasings (no double quotes, no backslashes, plain coaching; adapt as needed):
- NASM lists landing on straight knees, a skipped or sloppy push-up, sagging hips in the plank and
  rushing among the most common burpee mistakes.
- A soft landing, with the knees bending as you land, cuts peak landing force by about a third
  compared with a stiff one.
- A push-up lifts roughly two-thirds to three-quarters of your body weight, so the plank and the
  push-up are where the chest, shoulders and triceps work.
- Reaching the arms overhead as you drive up adds height and helps you finish tall.
- CrossFit's coaching notes that knees caving in on the jump-in usually comes from landing the feet
  too close together; land with the whole foot down and the knees in line with the toes.
- CrossFit's coaching: if the jump comes up short and you stay bent forward, look ahead or reach for
  a target so the hips and knees open fully.
- In one study, 30-second all-out sets of burpees pushed heart rate to about 85% of maximum, a
  vigorous effort; sets of 10 also cost more oxygen than heavy squats or deadlifts in another.
- The burpee does not need to be fast to work (ACE); keep a pace at which every rep stays clean.
