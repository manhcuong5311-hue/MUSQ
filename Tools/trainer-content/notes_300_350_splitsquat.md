# Legs 300-350: split squats and Bulgarian split squats (2026-09-26)

Seven exercises from the quads series (316-322): `spec_300_350_splitsquat.py`
holds the copy, `Tools/fault-review/faults_300_350_splitsquat.swift.txt` the
ghosts. The header of the spec lists the full source citations; this file maps
each claim in the copy to them and records what the models show.

## Shared facts about the models

- All seven keep the **left foot forward for both reps** (no leg switch) and
  are framed from the left, the lifter facing screen-left: yaw -1.3 (barbell,
  dumbbell, front-foot-elevated, rear-foot-elevated) or -1.0 (both Smith lifts
  and the barbell Bulgarian). So front-leg cues track the `_L` joints, the
  back knee `patella_R`, the bench foot `foot_R`.
- Tempo: two reps in 8 s, 1.75 s down, 0.33 s pause, 1.42 s up. Bottoms at
  2.0 s and 6.0 s (6.04 s for the Smith Bulgarian).
- Rig torso (neck to pelvis) 0.59 m, thigh 0.44 m, shin 0.40 m, ankle to ball
  of the foot 0.15 m, standing knee height ~0.48 m, shoulder joints 0.39 m
  apart.
- Barbell and Smith grips are 0.78 m, twice the shoulder-joint width, so the
  copy says "hands wider than your shoulders". The bar sits ~11 cm behind and
  1-3 cm below the neck joint: across the upper traps (high bar).
- Dumbbell handles run front to back (palms in), arms straight, hands ~0.32 m
  from the midline beside the hips.

## Barbell Split Squat

Model: high bar, ankles ~0.90 m apart front to back and ~0.23 m side to side
(hip width), front foot flat, back foot on its ball with the heel up the
whole clip. Hips sink 27 cm straight down (≤3 cm forward) to front knee 90°,
back knee 87°, front thigh just above parallel, front shin ~9° forward with
the knee ~9 cm behind the ball of the foot, back kneecap ~12 cm off the floor.
Trunk 3-6°.

Claims and sources:
- Quadriceps first, glutes close behind (HI 0.86 / MOD 0.62): Mausehund 2019
  (high-bar barbell split squat, 6-8RM to failure: vastus lateralis 95-101%
  MVIC, gluteus maximus 71-79%, H:Q ratio 0.69, i.e. quadriceps dominant);
  Escamilla 2025 (dumbbell lunge without a stride = split squat, long step:
  vasti 62-63% MVIC on the ascent, gluteus maximus ~48% on the ascent without
  a stride). ExRx files all five pages under the quadriceps (target).
- Hamstrings MOD 0.40 and gluteus medius MOD 0.40, level: the studies
  disagree on their order. Mausehund: biceps femoris 62.3% > gluteus medius
  46.2% MVIC; Escamilla 2025 without a stride, long step: hamstrings 21-23%
  vs gluteus medius 21% (15-21% across both steps); Stastny 2015 (one
  dumbbell, 5RM): gluteus medius >40% MVIC in trained lifters with the biceps
  femoris the lowest muscle measured.
- No split squat study measured the adductor magnus (Escamilla 2025 measured
  the adductor longus, ~21% MVIC, level with the hamstrings), so the
  adductors are listed as stabilisers rather than ranked in the panel; ExRx
  lists the adductor magnus as a synergist.
- Adductors, erector spinae, calves, core as stabilisers: ExRx (synergist
  adductor magnus and soleus; dynamic stabiliser gastrocnemius; stabiliser
  erector spinae).
- Bar high on the traps keeps the torso upright: Glassbrook 2017 (back
  squats: high bar more upright and quadriceps-led, low bar more lean) —
  extrapolated to the split squat and worded "in back squats".
- Torso upright; leaning with a bar is hard to balance and lengthens the
  lever on the lower back: McCurdy 2017 (forward lean not recommended with a
  bar on the shoulders; high centre of mass increases the difficulty).
- Knee tracks the foot; forward travel is normal: ExRx ("knees point same
  direction as feet"), McCurdy 2017 (valgus an error; the lead knee about
  over the toe line, slightly in front acceptable), Fry 2003 (knee travel
  appropriate). A caving knee "often" shows the hip losing control: Powers
  2010 says hip, pelvis and trunk control can affect knee mechanics, not
  that it always explains valgus (foot and ankle factors also play a part).
  The mistake says "at the bottom or on the way up" rather than naming one
  phase, since no cited source times it; the ghost is held at the bottom.
- Depth to about parallel, back knee just off the floor: ExRx ("almost makes
  contact with floor"). The why says "a long range", not "full range" (at
  about parallel the front hip is near 90°, short of its full flexion), and
  that drifting forward shortens the rep and leaves the back knee high,
  which is what the ghost draws (hips 11 cm higher and 6 cm forward, the
  front knee only ~2.5 cm forward); it no longer claims the knee is pushed
  past the foot.
- Setup: bar racked at upper-chest height so the lifter can step under it
  and stand up with it (ExRx Smith Split Squat: bar at upper-chest height;
  the same height is used for the free bar).
- Heel down, weight through heel and mid-foot: McCurdy 2017 (lead foot weight
  mid-foot or near the heel), Fry 2003.
- Comparison TORSO TIPS FORWARD: McCurdy 2017.

## Dumbbell Split Squat

Model: the barbell split squat's legs and trunk (3-6°) with dumbbells at the
sides, palms in, arms straight.

- Same activation as the barbell version: Wu 2020 found no difference
  between barbell, dumbbell and vest loading in lunges, but it measured only
  thigh and lower-leg muscles (quadriceps, hamstrings, tibialis anterior,
  gastrocnemius), and its abstract does not say how the loads were matched.
  The glute rows rest on Escamilla 2025, which itself used dumbbells.
- Escamilla 2025 is the closest match (dumbbells, feet stationary): without
  a stride, long step, vasti 62-63% MVIC, gluteus maximus ~48%, hamstrings
  21-23%, gluteus medius and adductor longus ~21%, gastrocnemius 20%. Its
  long step (shin about vertical at the bottom) matches this model's stance
  (front shin ~9° forward). Same ACT_FLAT rows and ranks as the barbell
  version.
- Comparison FRONT KNEE CAVING IN: McCurdy 2017 lists knee valgus among the
  errors; ExRx; Powers 2010.
- Grip copy: dumbbells at the sides keep the centre of mass low and steady
  the lift (McCurdy 2017: "the dumbbells lower the center of mass which can
  improve the stability"); "barbell and dumbbell loading have drawn similar
  thigh and calf muscle activity" in lunges: Wu 2020 (glutes not measured).
- Torso copy (TORSO_DB, shared with the front- and rear-foot-elevated
  versions): a deliberate forward lean adds glute and hamstring work
  (Farrokhi 2008, lunge: hip extensor impulse, gluteus maximus and biceps
  femoris EMG up with the trunk forward; Aygun-Polat 2025, Bulgarian split
  squat: trunk flexion raised gluteus maximus, biceps femoris and rectus
  femoris activation), so the copy no longer says leaning "shifts" work away
  from the quadriceps. The mistake is folding forward "without meaning to",
  which keeps it consistent with the library's Bulgarian Split Squat (Lean)
  and Lunge (Lean) entries, where the lean is deliberate. Harder to balance
  on a split stance: McCurdy 2017 (forward lean difficult to control with
  the split stance); the lower back holding the trunk: mechanics (a longer
  trunk lever).

## Smith Machine Split Squat

Model: the same legs; the Smith bar runs straight up and down above the start
hip line, midway between the front ankle and the back ball of the foot; trunk
9° at the top, 6° at the bottom.

- Activation a step lower than free weights (Quadriceps 0.82, Gluteus
  Maximus 0.58, Gluteus Medius LOW 0.34, Hamstrings LOW 0.32): no Smith split
  squat EMG study found. Ranked from the free split squat and lowered because
  the free squat drew 43% more EMG averaged over the muscles than the Smith
  squat, the biceps femoris 26% more (Schwanbeck 2009: 0.40 / 1.26 ≈ 0.32),
  trunk and soleus activity were lowest on the Smith squat (Anderson & Behm
  2005), and McCurdy 2017 notes the Smith machine reduces frontal-plane
  muscle activation (gluteus medius 0.40 -> 0.34). Uncertain in size.
  Adductors, calves, core as stabilisers.
- Stance cue: the fault is the heel, not the knee. Why: where the front foot
  sits decides how the work is shared, closer under the bar more quadriceps,
  further ahead more glutes and hamstrings (ExRx Smith Split Squat: forward
  foot further forward emphasises the gluteus maximus, closer under the bar
  the quadriceps; Escamilla 2025: the long step drew more hamstring and
  gluteus maximus activity on the descent, the short step without a stride
  more vasti on the ascent; Schütz 2014: step length and shin angle change
  the knee and hip loading). Forward knee travel is fine (Fry 2003); the
  mistake is a foot so close that the heel peels up (ExRx Smith Single Leg
  Split Squat: keep the foot flat). The correct text puts the front foot far
  enough ahead that the whole foot stays flat, the bar over the middle of the
  stance, which is what the model shows (front ankle ~0.40 m ahead of the
  bar, about half the 0.90 m stance). Comparison FRONT FOOT TOO CLOSE matches.
- Depth cue DEPTH_SMITH: the rails stop the hips drifting forward (the model's
  pelvis moves at most 3 cm forward), so the mistake is only stopping high.
- Bar cue mistake: the bar set low on the back of the shoulders, the chest
  tipping and the hips sliding back under the fixed bar (the ghost keeps the
  bar on its track).
- Torso cue (hips slide back behind the fixed bar when leaning): mechanics of
  a vertical track; the lean shifting work from the front knee toward the
  hips: Farrokhi 2008 (hip extensor impulse up with the trunk forward) and
  Fry 2003 (holding the knees back cut knee torque and raised hip torque and
  trunk lean). The label reads Chest up, hips under, so it names the chest
  its dot sits on.
- Setup "bar at upper-chest height" and "turn the bar to unhook it": ExRx
  Smith Split Squat ("With bar upper chest height", "Disengage bar by
  rotating bar back").

## Front-Foot-Elevated Split Squat

Model: dumbbells at the sides; the whole front foot on a 10 cm step; front
knee 175° -> 63°, front hip 141° -> 80°, the front thigh ~3° past parallel,
the front shin 23° forward with the kneecap over the ball of the foot, back
kneecap ~10 cm off the floor. Hips drop 37 cm (27 cm in the flat version) and
travel ~18 cm forward toward the front foot. Trunk 4-9°.

- **No EMG study of the front-foot-elevated split squat was found.** Ranked
  as the flat split squat (Mausehund 2019, Escamilla 2025: quadriceps 0.86,
  hamstrings and gluteus medius 0.40) with only the gluteus maximus raised to
  MOD 0.66 for the extra depth (Caterisano 2002, bilateral back squats: the
  gluteus maximus share of the concentric EMG rose with depth while the
  biceps femoris and vasti shares did not change; so the quadriceps stay at
  0.86). Adductors are listed as stabilisers, not ranked: no split squat
  study measured the adductor magnus, and the adductor growth claim in the
  depth cue (Kubo 2019) is a training result from bilateral full vs half
  squats, not EMG.
- Depth copy ("the step adds range; in squat studies, going deeper brought
  the glutes in more, and full squat training built more glute and adductor
  muscle than half squats"): model numbers; Caterisano 2002 (bilateral back
  squats: gluteus maximus share 16.9 / 28.0 / 35.4% partial / parallel /
  full); Kubo 2019 (bilateral full vs half squat training). The copy names
  them as squat studies because no front-foot-elevated study exists; the
  comparison note says the same ("the range where squat studies found the
  glutes working harder"). "If your knees are sensitive, build up to the
  depth": Escamilla 2008 (patellofemoral force and stress rise with knee
  flexion).
- Step cue (knee travels to about over the toes, expected while the heel
  stays down): model; Fry 2003.
- Comparison STOPPING SHORT: consistent with the depth cue.

## Rear-Foot-Elevated Split Squat

Model: dumbbells at the sides; back foot laces-down on a flat bench, pad top
0.48 m (knee height); front ankle 0.75 m in front of the bench edge; feet
~0.24 m apart; front knee 154° at the top (never fully straight) -> 79°, the
front thigh about parallel, the front shin 14° forward with the kneecap ~7 cm
behind the ball of the foot; the back knee stays bent (71-81°) and its
kneecap bottoms out ~16 cm off the floor ("a hand's width" in the copy).
Trunk 6° -> 12°.

Difference from the existing **Bulgarian Split Squat** (same exercise, model
`BulgarianSplitSquatUpright`, framed front-on): the old model stands wider
(~0.32 m vs ~0.24 m), a little shorter front to back (0.77 m vs 0.83 m
ankle to ankle), straightens the front knee further at the top (167° vs
154°), leans more at the bottom (to 20° vs 12°) and drops faster (~1.2 s)
with a longer hold. Depth, bench height and front-foot placement match. The
new copy differs from the old entry in two places, both evidence-led: it adds
a Hamstrings row (see below) and swaps the old "front foot" cue for a rear
foot cue (laces on the bench), which this model shows clearly; the front heel
point is kept in the setup and depth text.

- Activation Quadriceps 0.86 > Gluteus Maximus 0.64 > Hamstrings 0.48 >
  Gluteus Medius 0.44 (from the flat 0.40 / 0.40, the hamstrings up by about
  Mausehund's 22% and the gluteus medius by less, as its rise was only a
  trend): Mausehund 2019 (RFESS: vastus lateralis ~95-101%,
  gluteus maximus 71-79%, biceps femoris 76.1% vs 62.3% in the split squat,
  gluteus medius 54.9% vs 46.2% as a trend; H:Q 0.83, still quadriceps
  dominant); DeForest 2014 (only biceps femoris higher than the split squat);
  Andersen 2014 (Bulgarian vs squat: biceps femoris 63-77% higher, vasti
  similar); McCurdy 2010 (more gluteus medius and hamstrings than a two-leg
  squat). Mackey & Riemann 2021 find the Bulgarian hip dominant by joint
  moments; EMG studies still rank the quadriceps first, so quadriceps stays
  primary.
- Depth why (DEPTH_BSS): the glutes, hamstrings and quadriceps are most
  active in the bottom third of the rep: McCurdy, Walker, Kelly & Polinski
  2021 (RFESS at 80% 1RM: gluteus maximus, vastus lateralis and medial and
  lateral hamstrings all highest in the bottom third of the hip range).
  "The front leg also carries more of the load than in a flat split squat":
  DeForest 2014 (higher front-leg peak vertical force in the rear-leg-
  elevated than the flat split squat). The earlier 85%/75% figures (McCurdy
  2017 quoting McCurdy 2010 and a lunge study) were dropped: second-hand,
  and the split depends on technique (Schütz 2014).
- Mausehund 2019, the main source, loaded a high bar (DeForest 2014 and
  Andersen 2014 do not name the device in their abstracts); the dumbbell
  version uses the same rows on Wu 2020's finding that barbell and dumbbell
  lunges draw similar thigh and calf activity (glutes not measured). Mausehund's RFESS rested the TOES of
  the rear foot on a tibia-length box, the contact McCurdy 2017 lists as an
  error; the models rest the laces, as the copy teaches.
- Laces on the bench, ankle pointed, toe-tip contact less stable; bench about
  knee height: McCurdy 2017. The back leg "steadies you and takes only a
  small share of the load": McCurdy 2017 (the top-of-foot support
  significantly reduces the load on the trail leg, which still adds
  support; the lead leg carries most of it). The earlier "stays passive" /
  "relax" wording was dropped. StrengthLog: knee-height bench about one long
  step behind.
- Setup order: dumbbells picked up first, then the back foot goes onto the
  bench (lifting dumbbells from the floor with one foot on a bench is
  awkward). McCurdy 2017 also allows setting the dumbbells on knee-height
  benches either side and picking them up once the rear foot is placed.
- Comparison FRONT KNEE CAVING IN: McCurdy 2017 (knee valgus and hip
  adduction among the common errors).

## Barbell Bulgarian Split Squat

Model: the rear-foot-elevated legs with the high bar; trunk 6° -> 12°; the bar
moves ~8 cm forward from top to bottom.

- Activation ACT_RFE, straight from Mausehund 2019's high-bar RFESS (with
  DeForest 2014 and Andersen 2014 for the hamstrings rise, and Mackey &
  Riemann 2021, also high bar, for joint moments). Erector spinae listed as a
  stabiliser for the back-loaded bar.
- Setup: bench inside a squat rack with the safety bars just under the bar's
  lowest point. McCurdy 2017: with a free bar and heavy relative loads the
  RFESS "should be performed inside a squat rack with spotters"; the high
  centre of mass makes torso sway hard to recover. Safety bars are the
  common solo equivalent of the spotters (practice, not from the paper).
- Comparison TORSO TIPS FORWARD: McCurdy 2017 (forward lean not recommended
  with the bar on the shoulders; spotters warranted with a barbell). Library
  difficulty ADVANCED fits that source.

## Smith Machine Bulgarian Split Squat

Model: the same legs inside the Smith machine, the bench behind the rails, the
bar on a vertical track above the start hip line; the trunk holds 12-14°
forward all clip (more than the free-bar version at the top).

- **No EMG study of the Smith Bulgarian was found** (Liao 2023 compared its
  load-velocity profile with the free-weight version, not muscle activity).
  Ranked from the free-weight Bulgarian a step lower, as the Smith split
  squat: Quadriceps 0.84, Gluteus Maximus 0.62, Hamstrings 0.42 (0.48 lowered
  by about the Smith squat's biceps femoris drop, Schwanbeck 2009, partly
  offset by this model's steadier 12-14° lean, Aygun-Polat 2025 and
  Farrokhi 2008 finding more hamstring activity with the trunk forward),
  Gluteus Medius LOW 0.34 (McCurdy 2017: the Smith machine reduces
  frontal-plane activation; Anderson & Behm 2005).
- Depth cue DEPTH_BSS_SMITH: DEPTH_BSS's why (McCurdy 2021) with a straight-
  down mistake, since the rails stop the hips drifting forward.
- Setup: bar at upper-chest height, bench behind the bar (ExRx Smith Single
  Leg Split Squat: "Place bench behind smith bar", "With bar upper chest
  height"). The model's bench sits inside the machine frame, its front edge
  ~39 cm behind the bar and rails, so the copy says "a short step behind
  it" rather than "behind the machine". Turn the bar to unhook it: ExRx
  ("Disengage bar by rotating bar back").
- Torso label: Stay tall (it names the chest its dot sits on; the TORSO_SMITH
  intro still covers the hips staying under the bar).
- Comparison STOPPING SHORT: McCurdy 2021 (RFESS: the glutes, hamstrings and
  quadriceps most active in the bottom third), consistent with the depth
  cue.

## Ghosts and when to still them

Framings -1.3: sagittal faults need no turn; the knee fault turns `faceOn`
(total -0.2). Framings -1.0: sagittal faults `.seen(-0.4)` (total -1.4), the
knee fault `.seen(0.8)` (total -0.2).

New pieces (documented in the swift text): `barSlidLow` (the Back Squat's bar
fault with 10° instead of 14° of lean; free bars only), `barLowOnTrack` (the
Smith version: the same lean and low hands with the hips and all they carry
0.13 torso lengths back, so the bar stays on its rails),
`hipsBehindFixedBar(_:)` (Smith lean: hips back by sin(angle) torso lengths
while the trunk tips, so the bar stays on its track), `frontFootTooClose`
(front foot 0.2 torso lengths closer and rocked 20° up about the toes, knee
re-seated), `rearFootOnToes` (bench foot turned 40° up about the toes, ankle
~15 cm higher). The dumbbell grip fault reuses `armsSwungForward(28)`; the
copy says the dumbbells swing forward in front of the thighs, which is what
that piece draws.

Checked with a Python port of `FaultGhost.solve` on the rig at the bottom
(2.0 s), against the Smith guide rods (z -0.056 on the split squat, 0.107 on
the Bulgarian, the bar's own line): `barLowOnTrack` keeps the bar within 3 mm
of its track (hands 6 cm lower, pelvis 7.7 cm back; `barSlidLow` would have
taken it 8 cm forward off the rods); the Smith depth ghost `shallow(0.18,
withBar: true)` lifts everything 10.7 cm straight up, the bar on its track
(the free-weight `ahead: 0.1` version would move it 5.9 cm forward);
`hipsBehindFixedBar(14)` keeps the bar within 2 mm while the pelvis goes
14 cm back; `frontFootTooClose` moves the front foot ~12 cm closer and lifts
the ankle ~7 cm, the shin tipping ~22° forward and the knee ending ~2 cm
behind the toe tips; the toes-tucked bench foot rises ~15 cm over the toes
(the back knee ~12 cm with it).

their bend-scaled ghosts still show ~30% at the top.

## Labels

Rows are written on the 0.14-0.86 scale and squeezed to 0.16-0.80 by
`spec_300_350.py`. On the three rear-foot-elevated models the bench-foot
label sits at trailing 0.68 (0.64 on screen), just under the foot on the
bench (foot_R at v 0.585-0.599), and the back-knee label at trailing 0.80
(0.747 on screen) below it: a short, nearly level leader to the back kneecap
at the bottom (v 0.72-0.74), not a long diagonal from the top right across
the glute and the near plate. The bench-foot label moved down from 0.48 on
screen because the fault screenshots showed the toes-perched ghost (the
ankle ~15 cm above the bench) drawn across the pill; at 0.64 the ghost knee
stays ~18-22 px (crop scale) above the pill. The dumbbell load label is
Dumbbells at sides (18 characters, starting at u 0.638) so it no longer
covers the near shoulder and upper arm at the start (u 0.51-0.56 on the
dumbbell split squat; the front-foot-elevated version's back edge just
reaches it at the start). The Smith torso labels name the chest their dot
sits on: Chest up, hips under (Smith split squat, ends at u 0.39, the head
at 0.47) and Stay tall (Smith Bulgarian, the head at 0.39).

On the flat models the front foot cues use the same lowest row (0.80 ->
0.747 on screen) on the left, between the front knee and the shoe, with a
nine- to eleven-letter label so the pill ends before the front shin at the
bottom (it may touch the shin by a point or two on the flat side-on
models).
Torso labels are short where the head drops toward the left edge at the
bottom (0.33-0.45 on u). Checked by drawing the laid-out pills and leaders
over the start and bottom screenshots; verify in the simulator.

## Uncertain

- Front-foot-elevated and both Smith variations have no direct EMG data;
  their rows are extrapolated as described above.
- The hamstrings vs gluteus medius order in the flat split squats is a tie
  because the three studies disagree (Mausehund: hamstrings higher;
  Escamilla: about level; Stastny: gluteus medius higher).
- The adductors appear only as stabilisers: split squat EMG studies measured
  the adductor longus, not the magnus.
- ExRx was read through Internet Archive copies of its pages (the live site
  blocks automated fetches); the archived pages carry the same text as
  quoted in the spec header and all file the five lifts under the
  quadriceps (Directory > Quads, target quadriceps).
- The safety-bar step in the barbell Bulgarian setup stands in for McCurdy
  2017's spotters; the paper names spotters, not safety bars.
- The high-bar vs low-bar lean evidence (Glassbrook 2017) is from bilateral
  back squats.
