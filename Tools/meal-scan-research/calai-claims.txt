- In an NIH test presented at NUTRITION 2026, photos of 102 meals (ingredients weighed to 0.1 g) were run through MyFitnessPal, Lose It!, Cal AI and Appediet. All four apps estimated calories about one-third too low, missing 250–345 kcal and about 30 g of fat per meal. Low-carb, high-fat (keto) meals did worst. The results are preliminary and not peer-reviewed. [stated] https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/07/260726015237.htm (2026-07-26)
- Per-app results of the NIH test, as mean calorie underestimates: Appediet 252 kcal, MyFitnessPal 327, Lose It! 333, and Cal AI 345, the largest. Cal AI and Lose It! also underestimated carbs by about 14 g, and all four apps underestimated fat by about 30 g. [stated] https://www.healio.com/news/primary-care/20260804/ai-photobased-calorietracking-tools-underestimate-them-by-33 (2026-08-04)
- In the NIH test, photos were submitted to the apps as images rather than shot live in each app. Any live depth sensing therefore likely played no part in Cal AI's result. [inferred] https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/07/260726015237.htm (2026-07-26)
- A large food database does not recover fat the camera cannot see. MyFitnessPal, with its very large database, still underestimated by 327 kcal, and every app missed about 30 g of fat per meal. [inferred] https://www.healio.com/news/primary-care/20260804/ai-photobased-calorietracking-tools-underestimate-them-by-33 (2026-08-04)
