- NutriBench showed large cross-cuisine gaps for GPT-4o with CoT (carbohydrate MAE 2.20 g for Nigerian meals vs 15.12 g for Sri Lankan), with higher-carbohydrate cuisines erring more. [stated] https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12843 (2026-03-03)
- Across studies, models seem to fall back on typical values: typical servings (Sweden), typical protein contents (Australia), fat over-estimated for lean Japanese hospital meals but under-estimated for high-fat keto meals (NIH). A dish cooked with more or less oil than the model's prior will likely be mis-scored in that direction. [inferred] https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC13029357/ (2026-03-18)
- A single photo, low reasoning effort and composition values from the model's own knowledge is the 'direct estimate' condition studied above; for comparable 2025-2026 models the expected meal-level energy error is roughly 20-35% MAPE, with fat and protein worse. Raising reasoning effort is unlikely to change this much. [inferred] https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26538 (2026-10-20)
- Ranked by measured effect, the biggest accuracy gains come from information the camera cannot see (user-entered amounts or weights, ingredient lists, oil/meat descriptors, food names), not from more reasoning, more calls or database retrieval. [inferred] https://vtechworks.lib.vt.edu/items/b67c2a61-f283-41ba-9602-9798306b9529 (2025-09-08)
- For Vietnamese mixed dishes, the closest evidence (Chinese OmniFood8K) implies image-only estimates run low by tens to over 100 kcal when oil is hidden, and US-only composition data can add density bias. A Vietnamese composition table plus recipe priors switched on by cuisine is the approach consistent with the evidence, but it has not been tested on Vietnamese food. [inferred] https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12911 (2026-07-14)
